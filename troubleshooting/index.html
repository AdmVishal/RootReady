Troubleshooting by Symptom

Start from the symptom: disk full, read-only filesystem, high load, high memory, I/O wait, service will not start, network down, multipath, LVM and boot failures.

Production safety

Read-only checks first. Never run a destructive command until you have confirmed the device, filesystem or host. When in doubt, take a snapshot or backup and open a change.

💾 Disk full

“No space left on device”, a filesystem at 100%, or applications failing to write.

df -hT
du -xh --max-depth=1 /var | sort -h | tail
df -i                # inodes
lsof +L1             # deleted files still held open

Why it happens: Large or runaway logs, deleted-but-open files, inode exhaustion, snapshots or thin pools filling up.

🔒 Filesystem read-only

“Read-only file system” when writing, or a mount that flipped to ro.

findmnt -no TARGET,OPTIONS /path
dmesg -T | grep -i -E 'remount|I/O error|EXT4-fs|XFS'
lsblk -o NAME,RO,MOUNTPOINT
multipath -ll       # SAN: all paths down?

Why it happens: The kernel remounted ext4 read-only after I/O or metadata errors, the XFS log shut down, a SAN path/LUN failed, or the mount was ro by configuration.

Full step-by-step →fsckSAN & multipath

📈 High load

Load average far above the CPU count; the server feels slow.

uptime; nproc
vmstat 1 5
top -b -n1 | head -20
iostat -xz 1 3

Why it happens: Load counts runnable and uninterruptible (D-state) tasks, so CPU saturation, I/O waits and memory pressure can all raise it.

Full step-by-step →SAR & performanceTop CPU/memory processes

🧠 High memory / OOM

Free memory near zero, heavy swapping, or processes killed by the OOM killer.

free -h
ps -eo pid,user,%mem,rss,cmd --sort=-%mem | head
vmstat 1 5          # si/so = swap in/out
dmesg -T | grep -i -E 'out of memory|oom-killer'

Why it happens: A leaking or oversized process, too little RAM or swap for the workload, or cgroup limits. Check the “available” column, not “free”.

Memory & swapFind top memory processes

⏳ High I/O wait

CPU is mostly idle but the system is slow; %wa is high in top or vmstat.

vmstat 1 5          # wa and b columns
iostat -xz 1 5       # await, %util, aqu-sz
pidstat -d 1 5       # per-process disk I/O
ps -eo state,pid,wchan:20,cmd | awk '$1=="D"'

Why it happens: Slow or saturated storage (disk, SAN path, NFS server), a backup or scan hammering the disk, or failing hardware.

Fix disk I/O bottlenecksiostat explained

⚙️ Service won’t start

systemctl start fails, or the service is “failed” or flapping after a reboot.

systemctl status SERVICE -l --no-pager
journalctl -xeu SERVICE --no-pager | tail -40
systemctl cat SERVICE
ss -ltnp             # port already in use?

Why it happens: Bad configuration, a missing ExecStart binary or user, a port conflict, permissions or SELinux, or an unmet dependency.

Full step-by-step →Managing servicessystemd chapter

🌐 Network not working

No connectivity, wrong IP, or names not resolving.

ip -br addr; ip route
nmcli device status
ping -c3 GATEWAY; ping -c3 8.8.8.8
getent hosts example.com

Why it happens: Interface down or misconfigured, wrong gateway or VLAN, DNS failure, firewall rules or a duplicate IP.

Network configuration & troubleshootingNetwork latency (Solaris Q&A)

🔑 SSH connection refused

“Connection refused”, a timeout, or a login that is rejected.

systemctl status sshd
ss -ltnp | grep ':22'
firewall-cmd --list-all
sshd -t; journalctl -u sshd -e --no-pager | tail

Why it happens: sshd not running or listening elsewhere, a firewall or security group, a bad sshd_config, SELinux port labels, or key/permission problems. Refused means a reply came back; a timeout usually means a firewall drop.

SSH & remote administrationFirewall

🔀 Multipath / SAN path failure

Paths shown as failed or faulty, a missing LUN, or I/O errors from SAN storage.

multipath -ll
multipathd show paths
lsscsi; ls /sys/class/fc_host/
dmesg -T | grep -i -E 'multipath|scsi|fail|I/O error' | tail

Why it happens: A failed HBA, cable, switch port or controller; zoning or masking changes; or stale paths after a LUN was removed.

Multipath explainedOrphan pathsSAN & multipath

🗂️ LVM won’t extend / missing

“Insufficient free space”, a VG or PV that is missing, or a filesystem that did not grow.

pvs; vgs; lvs
vgs -o +vg_free_count,vg_free
lsblk
lvextend -r -L +5G /dev/VG/LV   # after checking free space

Why it happens: No free extents in the VG, a new disk not yet added with pvcreate and vgextend, or lvextend run without -r so the filesystem was not resized.

Extending LVMExtend an LV with no free spaceLVM components

🚫 Boot failure / rescue mode

Drops to emergency shell, kernel panic, or stuck at GRUB or dracut.

systemctl --failed
journalctl -xb -p err --no-pager
cat /etc/fstab; findmnt --verify
ls /boot; grubby --default-kernel

Why it happens: A bad /etc/fstab entry, a missing initramfs or kernel, a failed filesystem check, or a storage driver or LVM problem after patching.

Boot stagesServer not booting after patchingGRUB2 / dracut fix

🛡️ SELinux denied

Permission denied although file modes and ownership look right.

getenforce
ausearch -m avc -ts recent
ls -lZ /path
restorecon -Rv /path

Why it happens: The file has the wrong SELinux context, a non-standard port or a boolean is not enabled. Fix the label or boolean; do not leave SELinux disabled.

Troubleshooting SELinux denialsSELinux port management

🧟 Zombie / D-state processes

Defunct (Z) processes pile up, or processes are stuck in uninterruptible sleep (D).

ps -eo pid,ppid,stat,wchan:20,cmd | awk '$3 ~ /^[ZD]/'
ps -o pid,ppid,cmd -p PPID
cat /proc/PID/stack   # root, kernel stack of a D process
dmesg -T | tail -30

Why it happens: Zombies are finished children whose parent has not called wait(); fix or restart the parent. D-state is usually blocked on storage or NFS and cannot be killed until the I/O returns.
